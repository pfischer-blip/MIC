How accurate is this?
  • Rate is measured against the phone's (or audio interface's) own sample clock. Every 10 ppm of clock error shifts the reading by 0.86 s/day. If you have a reference (for example a watch you've had checked on a proper timegrapher), enter the difference as the clock correction.
  • Amplitude depends entirely on the lift angle you enter. Look up the lift angle for your movement; 52° is only a common default. Formula (same as the open-source tg timegrapher): amplitude = lift angle ÷ (2·sin(π·t·bph/7200)), where t is the time from the unlock sound to the drop sound.
  • Beat error is half the difference between the tick-to-tock and tock-to-tick intervals.
  • Clamp the watch firmly against the piezo, keep the room quiet, and don't touch the setup while it measures.
Connecting a piezo to an iPhone
  • Best: USB-C audio interface with an instrument (Hi-Z) input. A bare piezo is very high impedance; plugged into a normal mic input it sounds thin and quiet with little bass. A Hi-Z input or a piezo preamp fixes that.
  • Headphones plug into the interface's headphone jack for live listening.
  • Bluetooth works, but a Bluetooth mic on iPhone runs over the hands-free (phone call) link, which is built for speech, not full-range audio. Fine for checking that something is making noise, not for detail.
  • After plugging in, pick it from the Input list. If it is not listed, unplug and replug it, then reload the page.
  • Keep the screen on while listening; iPhone may stop the mic when the screen locks.
