What this can and can't do
  • Uses Google's YAMNet model, trained on AudioSet (521 everyday sound types). It runs entirely on your phone; no audio leaves it. The first use downloads about 26 MB.
  • It does not reach 90% on everything. Google reports a balanced mean average precision of 0.306 across all 521 classes. An independent test on the ESC-50 dataset got 92% for the animal category overall, but some individual sounds scored far lower.
  • A contact mic hears vibration, not air. Voices, birds and gunshots in the air will be faint or missing through a piezo. Use the phone's built-in mic (pick it under Input) for those. Tools, engines and insects in wood couple well into a contact mic.
  • For a specific sound you need at 90%, train the Sound detector below with your own examples. It measures its own accuracy.

How to train it
  • Add a sound called Background first and record 5 or more examples of normal, quiet conditions. The detector uses it to tell when none of your sounds is present.
  • Add each sound you want detected, and record 5 to 10 one-second examples of each. Make the sound during the recording.
  • Record the examples the way you'll use it: same mic placement, same gain.
  • Measured accuracy is a leave-one-out test: each example is hidden in turn and classified using all the others. Above 90% on your examples is a good sign, but real conditions can be harder. Add more varied examples if it drops.

How accurate is this?
  • Rate is measured against the phone's (or audio interface's) own sample clock. Every 10 ppm of clock error shifts the reading by 0.86 s/day. If you have a reference (for example a watch you've had checked on a proper timegrapher), enter the difference as the clock correction.
  • Amplitude depends entirely on the lift angle you enter. Look up the lift angle for your movement; 52° is only a common default. Formula (same as the open-source tg timegrapher): amplitude = lift angle ÷ (2·sin(π·t·bph/7200)), where t is the time from the unlock sound to the drop sound.
  • Beat error is half the difference between the tick-to-tock and tock-to-tick intervals.
  • Clamp the watch firmly against the piezo, keep the room quiet, and don't touch the setup while it measures.
